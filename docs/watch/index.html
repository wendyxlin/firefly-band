Firefly Watch Concept
Design review · watch direction

Firefly, worn as a watch

Everything Firefly needs, disguised as an ordinary dress watch. The crown is the Find button, a small pusher is Safety, the mother-of-pearl dial glows through its hour markers, and the antennas hide inside the leather strap. Friends nearby connect directly with no network; anyone farther away, 6 miles or more, is tracked over low-power cellular.

A proposal only. The concept page, website, video and app stay as they are until this is approved.

Where everything hides

Every Firefly part has a watch part to hide behind

Firefly needsHidden asHow it works
Button 1 · FindThe crown at 3 o'clockKnurled gold crown with a small opal cabochon on its tip. Press it in like setting a watch. It's the button your thumb finds first.
Button 2 · SafetyA pusher at 2 o'clockSmooth, slimmer than the crown, ringed in red enamel, so it feels and looks different. Its position also makes it hard to press by accident when your wrist bends.
The glowing stoneMother-of-pearl dial, lit markersTwelve LEDs under the dial shine through a small translucent window under each hour marker. Finding: the marker pointing toward your friend glows and pulses faster as you get closer. SOS: the dial pulses red. Check-in delivered: one green sweep.
AntennasInside the strap, both halvesFlexible antennas laminated between the leather layers, outside the metal case: LoRa on the 12 o'clock side, cellular and GPS on the 6 o'clock side. That fixes the gold-blocks-the-radio problem, so the case can be real metal.
Long-range trackingOne extra chip on the boardA cellular IoT chip with built-in GPS (Nordic nRF9151) sends your position over the low-power LTE-M and NB-IoT networks when friends are out of radio range. See "Range" below.
BatteryUnder the dialA round cell about 30 mm across and 3 mm thick, 185–210 mAh. Cells this size are sold off the shelf for wearables. No clasp box any more.
Vibration motorThe casebackAn 8 mm coin motor pressed against the skin, so the hot-or-cold pulse is felt and never heard.
ChargingTwo pins on the casebackA magnetic cable snaps onto the back. No port to seal.
Telling timeReal handsA tiny stepper motor driven by the same chip moves the hands, like a hybrid smartwatch. A watch that doesn't tell time would give the disguise away.
Range · requirement: at least 6 miles, even with a weak signal

Six miles comes from networks, not from a stronger radio

No wrist-sized radio reaches 6 miles straight to another wrist in a city. Bodies, buildings and a tiny antenna cap that link at about 50–150 m in a packed club and 300 m–1 km outdoors. So the watch keeps the direct link for friends nearby and reaches everyone farther away through networks. The cellular layer is what guarantees 6 miles; the other two are free fallbacks on hardware the watch already has.

Watch to watch (LoRa)50–150 m indoors, 300 m–1 km outdoors, more with relaysWorks with zero bars: no phone, tower or internet. Finding, "come find me", help and SOS.Already in the design (SX1262).
Cellular LTE-M / NB-IoT · core6 miles and beyond: anywhere with IoT coverageBuilt for weak signal. NB-IoT allows about 164 dB maximum coupling loss and LTE-M about 161 dB, roughly 20 dB more than a phone's normal LTE link, so it often gets through where a phone shows no bars.Nordic nRF9151: LTE-M, NB-IoT and GPS in one 12.1 × 11.1 × 1.2 mm package. One-time data: 1NCE sells 10 years and 500 MB for $10 plus $1 for the SIM.
Amazon Sidewalk · free fallbackHundreds of metres to about half a mile from any Echo or Ring deviceUses the same LoRa radio. Amazon says it covers about 90% of the US population, and moving devices roam between bridges.No new parts: Nordic supports Sidewalk on the nRF52840 with the SX1262. A LoRa message is limited to 19 bytes; a compressed position fits.

Any nearby iPhone or Android phone reports its position, end-to-end encrypted.No new parts: Apple Find My (needs an Apple MFi license) or Google Find Hub. Coverage varies by neighbourhood. Before committing, the team should check LTE-M and NB-IoT coverage (and which carriers the data plan uses) and Sidewalk density in the places the group actually goes out.

Feasibility check · October 27

Possible to build, with three fixes

Each part of the design was checked against real parts and published specs. Nothing needs technology that doesn't exist. These are the open issues:

ItemVerdictEvidence and what to do
6-mile trackingFeasibleVia cellular (nRF9151) where there's LTE-M or NB-IoT coverage, with Sidewalk and phone crowds as fallbacks. A direct watch-to-watch link can't do 6 miles, and the page no longer claims it.
BatteryFeasible, tightRound cells this size exist off the shelf (Grepow: 185 mAh in about 30 × 2.9 mm). Estimate: about 41 h on a typical night and about 12 h on a heavy one. Cellular updates every 2 minutes take about 0.7 h off that. Use a 200–210 mAh cell (about 3.3 mm) to keep a margin above 12 h, and cap GPS searching indoors.
WeightOver by 1–2 gAbout 31 g as drawn: titanium case 12 g, crystal 2.5, battery 3.6, electronics with GPS and cellular 3.5, strap and buckle 6.7, the rest 3. Fix: a 32 mm case and a slimmer 2.2 mm strap bring it to about 28–29 g. Steel or gold-plated brass cases are not possible under 30 g.
Thickness and fitFeasibleStack: caseback 0.8, motor 2.0, battery 3.0, board 0.8, tallest parts 2.0, dial and hands 1.5, crystal 1.2, so about 12 mm. The nRF52840, SX1262, nRF9151 and passives fit on a 27 mm round board using both sides. The prototype on dev boards stays about 15 mm.
Hands driven by the chipProvenHybrid smartwatches already ship microcontroller-driven hands (for example Frederique Constant and MyKronoz ZeTime).
Antennas in the strapProven idea, needs engineeringBand-embedded antennas are an established approach (several patents). A 915 MHz quarter-wave antenna is about 8 cm, which fits one strap half. It needs a reliable contact through the lug and tuning on a real wrist.
Light through the dialNeeds a prototypeMother-of-pearl only lets light through when it's very thin and fragile. The safer build is a small translucent window under each hour marker. Test both on samples.
Pointing toward a friendOutdoors onlyNeeds both GPS fixes plus a compass, and the compass needs calibrating inside a metal case. Indoors it falls back to hot or cold by signal strength.
CertificationPlan for itFCC Part 15, carrier approval for the cellular radio (the nRF9151 is pre-certified, which helps), Amazon's Sidewalk qualification, and Apple MFi or Google's program for crowd finding. Budget months, not weeks.
In the dark

Two controls, used like a watch

The gestures are the same as today's two buttons, so the firmware and app don't change.

Crown · Find

  • pressFind your friends. The lit marker points the way and pulses faster as you get closer.
  • 2×"Come find me" to the group.
  • hold"I'm okay" check-in. The dial answers with a green sweep when it's delivered.
  • 3×"I'm home" for the end-of-night roll call.

Pusher at 2 o'clock · Safety

  • pressA discreet "I need help" to the friends you choose. One small buzz, no light.
  • 2×Cancel within the 5–10 second grace window.
  • hold 3 sSOS to the whole group. The dial pulses red so you know it went.

Trade-offs

What the watch shape costs us

Thickness

The production design is 34 mm across and about 12 mm thick, like a sturdy everyday watch. The dev-board prototype needs about 15 mm, because the off-the-shelf boards are square and stacked.

Weight

About 31 g as drawn, 1–2 g over the limit. A 32 mm titanium case under gold PVD and a slimmer strap bring it to about 28–29 g. Steel or gold-plated brass cases would be 40–45 g.

New parts

Lit markers need 12 LEDs instead of 4, and real hands need a small stepper motor. Pointing toward a friend also needs the compass and GPS from the brief, so it works well outdoors and only roughly indoors.
