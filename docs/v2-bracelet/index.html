Why it exists

The group chat goes quiet right when it matters.

We asked friends what goes wrong on a night out. The same three things came up every time.

"My phone died and I couldn't find anyone."

Phones die, signal drops in basements, and texts arrive twenty minutes late.

"I didn't want to make a scene to ask for help."

Unlocking a phone to call someone is obvious. Pressing a watch pusher isn't.

"Did everyone get home?"

Nobody wants to text "home?" five times at 3 am. One press should answer it.

The watch

Two buttons. Nothing else to learn.

3
Crown · 3 o'clock

I'm Home

Press once when you're through the door. Your circle gets a check-in and your dial sweeps green when it's delivered.

  • Pressed by mistake? Press again within 5 seconds to cancel.
2
Red-ringed pusher · 2 o'clock

SOS

Hold for 3 seconds. Your circle and your backup contact get your live location, and keep getting it until you're safe.

  • Grace window of 5 to 10 seconds: press twice to cancel.
  • The dial pulses red so you know it went.

Everything else happens on its own. If you drift outside the group's radius, the watch buzzes and the marker pointing toward your circle glows lavender. Finding a friend and "Come Find Me" live in the app.

The app

Your circle, for one night.

Start a night, add your people, set how far apart is too far. Everything ends when you tap End night.

Separation alerts

Pick a buddy radius from 10 to 30 m and an alert delay of 1, 2 or 5 minutes, so a bathroom trip doesn't set it off.

Find a friend up close

Ultra-wideband between watches gives a distance to the metre. No map to squint at, just walk until the number drops. Their dial glows so they know.

Who's home?

Every crown press lands here. See who's still out, and nudge them without a single text.

Come Find MeSend your position to the circle with one tap when you're the one who's lost.

Range

Six miles and more, even with one bar.

The watch picks the best way to reach your circle on its own. It doesn't need your phone to send an SOS or an I'm Home.

Privacy

Shared for the night, then gone.

Only your circle

Location goes to the people you added tonight, no one else. There is no public map and no follower list.

Ends with the night

Tap End night and sharing stops. Nothing keeps tracking you through the week.

Encrypted end to end

Positions are encrypted on the watch, so the networks that carry them can't read them.

FAQ

Questions

Why only two buttons?

Because those are the two things you need without looking: telling people you're home, and asking for help. Everything else is either automatic (separation alerts) or something you'd do on your phone anyway (finding a friend, Come Find Me).

What if I press one by accident?

I'm Home cancels if you press the crown again within 5 seconds. SOS needs a 3-second hold, then gives you a 5 to 10 second window to cancel with two presses before anyone is alerted.

Does it work if my phone is dead?

Yes. SOS and I'm Home go out from the watch itself, over its own radio or cellular connection. Your friends see them in their app.

How far away can my friends find me?

Up close (0 to 30 m) the app gives a distance to the metre. Across a venue or a few blocks it uses watch-to-watch radio. Beyond a kilometre, the built-in cellular link covers six miles and more, and it is designed for weak, one-bar signal.

Is there a monthly fee?

No. A small amount of cellular data is bundled with the watch for its lifetime. Location messages are tiny, so it lasts years.

How long does the battery last?

About 13 hours on a heavy night with lots of finding, separation alerts and cellular check-ins, and well over a day on a quiet one. The magnetic charger snaps onto the caseback.

Does my circle need an Afterglow too?

Friends without a watch can join a night in the app and see check-ins and SOS alerts. Up-close distance and watch-to-watch radio need a watch on both wrists.
